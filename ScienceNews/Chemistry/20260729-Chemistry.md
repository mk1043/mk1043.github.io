Naked mole rats ( Heterocephalus glaber ) have become unlikely celebrities of modern biology. Their exceptional longevity, resistance to cancer and ability to stay healthy as they age have made them leading models for biogerontology, the study of the biological processes of ageing 1 . Yet the feature that first attracted scientific attention to them was arguably even more unusual. These subterranean rodents live in large colonies centred on a single breeding queen, creating ‘eusocial’ systems that resemble those of ants, termites and honeybees more closely than those of any other mammal 2 . How these colonies maintain stable reproductive hierarchies has remained one of the central questions in the evolution of eusociality.
